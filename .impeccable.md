## Design Context

### Users
- Primary users are high-frequency task managers using Task Center while away from their desk.
- They often open the app in fragmented moments: commuting, walking between meetings, waiting in line, or quickly checking status between conversations.
- The product must support fast scanning, low-friction updates, and confident decision-making on a small screen.
- This is a productivity tool, not a showcase site. Users come here to understand what matters now and act quickly.

### Use Cases
- See what is due today, what is overdue, and what has been deferred.
- Quickly open a task, understand its state, and take the next action.
- Create or edit a task on mobile without feeling like filling out a heavy admin form.
- Adjust time, recurrence, defer, cancel, or complete tasks while on the go.
- Review plan/history with minimal taps and strong information hierarchy.

### Brand Personality
- Professional
- Calm
- Clear

Emotional goals:
- The interface should feel trustworthy, composed, and under control.
- It should reduce mental clutter rather than impress with visual tricks.
- It should feel fast, intentional, and forgiving on a mobile device.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Mobile-first productivity interface.
- Refined, lightweight, and tactile rather than decorative.
- Prefer strong hierarchy, breathable spacing, and obvious tap targets over visual novelty.
- Keep the overall feeling modern and polished, but restrained.
- Avoid turning the app into a marketing page, concept shot, or overly expressive design exercise.

References to emulate:
- High-quality productivity/mobile utility apps where hierarchy is instantly legible.
- Interfaces that feel editorially composed but operationally efficient.

Anti-references:
- Loud startup landing-page aesthetics.
- Generic AI dashboard tropes.
- Neon-on-dark, gradient-heavy, glow-heavy styling.
- Over-carded layouts, nested containers, decorative charts, and “design for design’s sake”.

### Theme Direction
- Both light and dark mode should be viable.
- Dark mode should feel calm and premium, not cyberpunk.
- Color should guide attention and state, not dominate the screen.

### Accessibility & Interaction Requirements
- Prioritize thumb-friendly tap targets and one-handed use.
- Keep critical actions easy to discover without crowding the screen.
- Preserve readability in outdoor/bright-light scenarios.
- Motion should be subtle and purposeful; never slow down task execution.
- Support reduced cognitive load first, expressive motion second.

### Design Principles
1. **Action first**
   - Every major screen should help users decide what to do next within seconds.
2. **Hierarchy over decoration**
   - Visual polish should improve scanning and prioritization, not add noise.
3. **Dense, but never cramped**
   - Mobile should carry meaningful information density while remaining touch-friendly.
4. **Progressive disclosure**
   - Show the most important state first; reveal secondary detail only when needed.
5. **Calm confidence**
   - The product should feel mature, dependable, and quietly sharp.

### Constraints for AI Design Work
- Do not redesign this into a flashy concept app.
- Do not sacrifice legibility or speed for novelty.
- Do not overuse cards, gradients, glassmorphism, decorative motion, or empty whitespace.
- Do not make typography or color choices that reduce long-session comfort.
- Treat recurrence/task editing as operational UI that must be obvious, lightweight, and robust.

### Current Improvement Priorities
- Sharpen the hierarchy of Today view on mobile.
- Make create/edit task flows feel lighter and more native to phone use.
- Improve detail sheet organization so actions do not compete equally.
- Reduce any remaining desktop-ish structure that feels heavy on a phone.
- Increase the sense of polish without losing speed or utility.
